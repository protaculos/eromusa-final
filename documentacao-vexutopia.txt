Quick Start
Get up and running with Vexutopia in three simple steps:

1
Get your API key
Sign in to your dashboard and create a new API key. Use test keys for development and live keys for production.

2
Create your first payment
Make an authenticated POST to /v1/payments — this both verifies your key works and gives you a real session ID to play with:

cURL
Copy
curl -X POST https://vexutopia.com/api/v1/payments \
  -H "X-API-Key: vex_test_your_api_key" \
  -H "Content-Type: application/json" \
  -d '{
    "amount": "20",
    "currency": "USD",
    "return_url": "https://yoursite.com/success"
  }'
The response body includes id (e.g. tx_…) and checkout_url. The customer goes to checkout_url to pay.

3
Retrieve a payment
Use the id returned in step 2 to look up the payment's current status:

cURL
Copy
# Replace tx_REPLACE_WITH_YOUR_ID with the id you got back in step 2.
curl https://vexutopia.com/api/v1/payments/tx_REPLACE_WITH_YOUR_ID \
  -H "X-API-Key: vex_test_your_api_key"

Required Headers
Parameter	Type	Description
X-API-Keyrequired	string	Your API key. Format: vex_test_xxx (test) or vex_live_xxx (live)
Content-Typerequired	string	Must be application/json for requests with a body
User-Agent	string	Strongly recommended for server-to-server integrations. Send a descriptive value identifying your app (e.g. "YourCompany-Integration/1.0"). See the note below.
Server-to-server: set a descriptive User-Agent
Our edge bot-protection can challenge or block requests whose User-Agent looks automated — many HTTP libraries send a generic default (or none), which may be flagged. If you see Cloudflare challenges or an Error 1010 on your API calls, it is caused by the User-Agent, not your IP address. Set an explicit, app-specific User-Agent header and the issue clears:

User-Agent: YourCompany-Integration/1.0

/v1/payments
Create a new payment session and get a hosted checkout URL

Request Body
Parameter	Type	Description
amountrequired	string	Decimal amount as a string — e.g., "20" or "19.99". Not cents.
currencyrequired	string	Three-letter ISO currency code. Supported: USD, EUR, GBP, CAD, AUD, JPY, BRL, MXN, ARS, RUB, CNY. Amounts are automatically converted to USDC at the current rate.
return_urlrequired	string	URL to redirect the customer after payment completes
customer_email	string	Customer email address
customer_name	string	Customer full name
customer_id	string	Your internal customer ID for reference

metadata	object	Custom key-value pairs (string values only)
country	string	Two-letter ISO 3166-1 alpha-2 country code of the customer. Used to filter region-restricted providers and to pre-select the customer's region on the hosted checkout page. Optional — if omitted, we fall back to IP-based geolocation; the customer can still change the region themselves.
locale	string	Optional UI language for the hosted checkout (BCP-47) — e.g. "fr", "de", "ja", "pt-BR", "ar". The whole checkout (amounts, payment options, card form, help, and the success screen) renders in this language, with right-to-left layout for Arabic and Hebrew. Forgiving about format: "FR", "fr-FR", "pt" (→pt-BR), and "zh-Hant" (→Traditional Chinese) all resolve. If omitted or unrecognized, the checkout follows the customer's region (e.g. Portuguese in Brazil) and otherwise falls back to English. You can also override per-link by appending ?locale= to the returned checkout_url. Supported: en, pt-BR, es, ru, zh-CN, zh-TW, fr, de, it, nl, pl, tr, ja, ko, hi, id, vi, th, uk, ro, cs, sv, el, ar, he.
direct	boolean	Skip our hosted checkout and return the upstream PSP payment link directly in the response (you redirect your customer there yourself). v1 supports BRL (PIX), ARS (C2C), and RUB (SBP). Your site must be approved for regional payments. See "Direct mode" below.
crypto	boolean	Route this payment to the direct-crypto checkout (customer pays in BTC, ETH, stablecoins, or other supported assets). Returns checkout_url on vexutopia.com/pay/crypto/… — not the multi-method /pay page. Minimum invoice USD 3 equivalent. See "Crypto (direct)" below.
payment_method	string	Required for CNY transactions to choose between Alipay and WeChat Pay. Accepts "ALIPAY" or "WXPAY". CNY without this defaults to Alipay. Per-order range: 1 – 1,000 CNY (~$0.15 – $138 USD) — orders outside this window are rejected with HTTP 422. Other rails ignore this field and pick a method automatically. Your site must be approved for China Pay.
card_payment	boolean	Force the card-payment rail (settlement in fiat) instead of letting routing pick. Use this when you want card processing for the customer rather than the crypto on-ramp fallback. Your site must be approved for card payments. Ignored if currency is CNY.

How it works
After creating a payment, redirect your customer to checkout_url. They will choose their preferred payment provider (MoonPay, Transak, etc.) and complete payment there. Once paid, Vexutopia fires a webhook to your webhook_url and your payout is sent instantly to your configured wallet. The checkout link expires after 1 hour.

United States card payments: Customers with US billing addresses pay on the same inline card page as everyone else (URL stays on vexutopia.com); the embedded iframe handles US too. The one US-specific requirement is that the customer must be on a US IP address — see the US card billing docs.

Wallet requirement: Payouts are delivered as USDC, PYUSD, or other ERC-20 stablecoins depending on the payment method used. Use a self-custody EVM wallet (MetaMask, Rabby, Phantom, etc.) — exchange deposit addresses (Binance, Coinbase, etc.) may assign different addresses per token, and funds sent to an unsupported token could be unrecoverable.

card vs card_payment — what's the difference?
Both let your customer pay with a card, but they settle the merchant in different assets and route through different rails. Use whichever fits your business model.

payment_method	What it is	You settle in	How to request
card	Crypto on-ramp. Customer pays with a card; an on-ramp partner converts the fiat to crypto.	USDT / USDC (ERC-20 stablecoin)	Default. No flag needed — just POST /v1/payments with no card_payment / crypto flags.
card_payment	Direct card processing through our card acquirer. Customer pays with a card; you receive fiat.	USD fiat (paid out to your bank)	Send card_payment: true. Requires per-site approval and a customer in a supported country (US, GB, EU, etc.).
If card_payment: true is sent for a country we don't cover with direct cards yet, you'll get 422 NO_PROVIDER_AVAILABLE — we don't silently fall back to the on-ramp.

Card payments — copy-paste example
To take card payments, send card_payment: true and redirect the customer to the returned checkout_url. Vexutopia's hosted card form collects the customer's card and billing details — do not send firstName, lastName, billing, or card details on this call.

cURL
Copy
curl -X POST https://vexutopia.com/api/v1/payments \
  -H "X-API-Key: vex_live_…" \
  -H "Content-Type: application/json" \
  -d '{
    "amount": "19.99",
    "currency": "USD",
    "customer_email": "buyer@example.com",
    "customer_name": "Jane Doe",
    "country": "US",
    "card_payment": true,
    "return_url": "https://yoursite.com/payment-status?order={order_id}",
    "webhook_url": "https://yoursite.com/api/vexutopia-webhook",
    "metadata": {
      "order_no": "your-internal-order-id",
      "user_id": "abc-123"
    }
  }'
Response (redirect the customer to checkout_url):

JSON
Copy
{
  "id": "tx_abc1234567890def",
  "status": "pending",
  "amount": "19.99",
  "currency": "USD",

Common mistake: sending firstName, lastName, or a billingobject on this call returns 400 CARD_PAYMENT_INTEGRATION — we collect those from the customer on the hosted form. US billing: handled automatically; the customer is silently routed to the secure US hosted card page. Embedded iframe: see Embedded Card Checkout for the on-site flow.

US card payments require a US IP address
For fraud prevention, US-billed card payments are restricted to customers connecting from a US IP address. Our US card acquirer treats geo-mismatched sessions (US billing from non-US IP) as high-risk: most are declined upstream, the small fraction that settle are disproportionately chargebacks, and you get billed for both. We hard-block these at the checkout layer so they never reach the card form.

What this means for your integration:

Sending country: "US" + card_payment: true on POST /v1/payments still succeeds — we don't IP-gate at create time (your server's IP isn't the customer's).
The gate fires when the customer actually visits the hosted card page from their browser. If they're not on a US IP, they see a clear message and a "back to checkout" button so they can pick a different method.
The /pay hosted checkout hides the Card Payments tile entirely when the customer's region resolves to US but their IP isn't US — they pick another rail (crypto on-ramp, regional, etc.) instead.
Test mode is exempt. API keys starting with vex_test_ bypass the gate so you can integration-test from any region.
API error code: US_IP_REQUIRED (HTTP 403). The response body includes detected_country for telemetry. The customer-facing message in the hosted UI is softer — "Card payments require a US connection."

Provider selection is automatic
Do not include a provider field in your request — it will cause an error. The payment provider is chosen by your customer on the checkout page. The provider field only appears in the response and webhooks after checkout to tell you which provider was used. Exception: when you set crypto: true, routing is fixed to the direct-crypto flow — customers do not pick a card/bank on-ramp provider for that session.

Multi-currency
Supported currencies: USD, EUR, GBP, CAD, AUD, JPY, BRL, MXN, ARS, RUB, CNY. For most rails, the amount is automatically converted to USDC at the live rate before the customer sees the checkout, and your payout is delivered in USDC. CNY routes through China Pay (Alipay/WeChat) and settles to your Vexutopia merchant balance instead of being converted on the fly. Unsupported currency codes return a 422 validation error.

Crypto (direct)
Pass crypto: true on POST /v1/payments to start a cryptocurrency checkout. The response checkout_url is on vexutopia.com/pay/crypto/<id> (embedded hosted flow; your customer completes payment there). This rail is not available as a choice on the standard multi-method /pay page — your backend must opt in per payment.

Fees — crypto processor percentage (in our configuration) plus 2% Vexutopia margin, shown as one combined rate on the invoice (see Payment Providers)
Minimum — USD 3 equivalent in your requested currency
Underpayment tolerance — sends within 5% of the quoted amount still settle as completed
return_url — may include {payment_id}, {order_id}, {transaction_id}, and {status}; these are replaced when the customer is sent back to your site
Webhooks — same events as other rails; payment_method is crypto
Example request
cURL
Copy
curl -X POST https://vexutopia.com/api/v1/payments \
  -H "X-API-Key: vex_live_your_api_key" \
  -H "Content-Type: application/json" \

    "amount": "10.00",

Telegram Stars (optional)
Telegram Stars can be offered in two ways. Both require a LIVE API key and Telegram Stars enabled under Dashboard → Settings (off by default).

Mode	How	Best for
Mixed checkout	Use POST /v1/payments as normal — Telegram Stars appears automatically at the top of the provider list if enabled	Most merchants — one API call covers all payment methods
Standalone	Use POST /v1/payments/telegram-stars to create a Stars-only checkout	Telegram bots or flows where Stars is the only payment option

Copy

Telegram initiates refunds unilaterally — you cannot prevent them. Revoke access to any digital goods when you receive this event. The payment status will also update to refunded if you poll GET /v1/payments/:id.

Chargeback fee — $40 per refund
Each Telegram Stars chargeback incurs a $40 platform fee. The fee is automatically deducted from your pending Stars payout balance at the time the chargeback is received — not at your next payout cycle. Your balance in Dashboard → Settings always reflects what you will actually receive. A running total of fees is shown for transparency. High chargeback rates may result in Telegram Stars being disabled for your account.

How it works
Redirect the customer to checkout_url. They open the Telegram invoice from the Vexutopia checkout page and complete payment in Telegram. If they don't have enough Stars, Telegram prompts them to buy more automatically. Webhooks fire on completion just like other payment methods.

Resend Webhook
POST
/v1/payments/:id/resend-webhook
Re-fire the payment.completed webhook for a completed transaction

Use this to recover from missed or failed webhook deliveries. The endpoint re-sends the payment.completed event to all your registered webhook endpoints and the webhook_url set at payment creation. Your handler should be idempotent — duplicate deliveries for an already-fulfilled payment should be a no-op on your side.

Example Request
cURL
Copy
curl -X POST https://vexutopia.com/api/v1/payments/tx_b84050910b493a1e/resend-webhook \
  -H "X-API-Key: vex_live_your_api_key"
JSON
Copy
{
  "ok": true,
  "transaction_id": "tx_b84050910b493a1e",
  "event": "payment.completed"
}
Only works on completed transactions. Returns 400 NOT_COMPLETED if the transaction is still pending, processing, or failed.

Rate limited to 60 requests per minute per API key (sliding window).

Requires a LIVE API key.

Payment Statuses
Status	Description
pending	Session created. Awaiting customer payment, or the customer has paid and we are awaiting upstream confirmation. Both pre-payment and post-payment in-flight transactions surface as "pending" — listen on the webhook (or poll) for the terminal state.
completed	Payment confirmed and payout sent.
failed	Payment failed or underpayment detected.
cancelled	Session was superseded by a different rail (e.g. customer abandoned card and paid via WeChat — see Regional payments correlation), or explicitly cancelled. The original session ID still appears as payment_id on the child's webhook so you can reconcile.
expired	Checkout link expired without payment (default 1 hour TTL).
refunded	Payment was refunded in full.
partially_refunded	Payment was refunded for less than the full amount.
Idempotency
Send an Idempotency-Key header on POST /v1/payments to make retries safe. If your service double-fires the same request (network retry, double-click, two tabs), reusing the same key returns the original response instead of creating a second payment session.

Generate a unique key per logical payment intent (a UUID is fine). Format: A-Z a-z 0-9 _ -, 1–255 chars.
Keys are scoped per API key and remembered for 24 hours.
Same key + identical body within 24h → original response replayed, with header Idempotent-Replayed: true.
Same key + different body → 409 IDEMPOTENCY_KEY_REUSED. Use a fresh key for new requests.
Server errors (5xx) are not cached, so a retry with the same key will be processed normally.
The header is optional — requests without it behave exactly as before.
Example

Copy
curl -X POST https://vexutopia.com/api/v1/payments \
  -H "X-API-Key: vex_test_your_api_key" \
  -H "Content-Type: application/json" \
  -H "Idempotency-Key: 8b1f2c4e-9d3a-4f7b-8c1d-2e3f4a5b6c7d" \
  -d '{

Regional payments: correlating the webhook to your original payment
When a customer pays via a regional rail (PIX in Brazil, C2C in Argentina, SBP in Russia), we settle the payment on a child transaction with its own ID. The original transaction we returned from POST /v1/payments is moved to CANCELLED with the reason “Superseded by [rail] regional payment”, and the payment.completed webhook is fired against the child.

To credit the customer in your system, correlate by payment_id (the original ID we returned to you) or by metadata.<your_field> if you passed one through.

Webhook payload (excerpt)
JSON
Copy
{
  "event": "payment.completed",
  "id": "tx_9fbd2dd90d27c04b",          // child tx (the regional settlement)
  "payment_id": "tx_68ede60fcaf92e24",   // original tx returned by POST /v1/payments
  "status": "completed",
  "amount": "98.47",
  "currency": "BRL",
  "processor": "regional",
  "payment_method": "pix",
  "settled_via": { "rail": "PIX", "amount": "98.47", "currency": "BRL" },
  "metadata": { "order_id": "1234" },
  "timestamp": "2026-05-08T01:33:43.656Z"

The same pattern applies if you also call GET /v1/payments/:id against the original ID — once the child has settled, the parent reflects status CANCELLED (superseded). Always trust the webhook's payment_id field as the authoritative back-reference.

Direct Integration
Skip checkout.vexutopia.com entirely. With direct: true, the response from POST /v1/payments is a Vexutopia-hosted payment page on the apex domain — you redirect your customer there from your own site (or open it in a popup, or anywhere else you choose).

Direct integration is a redirect-target change only. Webhooks, status polling, fees, rolling reserve and partner attribution behave identically to the hosted flow.

When to use Direct vs. Hosted
Use the hosted page
You want customers to choose between several methods
You want our trust badges, brand polish, and method discovery
You don't want to manage redirects yourself
Use direct integration
You already have your own checkout UI
The customer has already chosen a method (e.g. a “Pay with PIX” button on your page)
You want to keep the customer on your domain until the redirect
Eligibility
Direct integration is gated by the same per-Site approval as the hosted flow. To use it you need:

An active Vexutopia merchant account with at least one approved Site.
Regional payments approval for the Site you're creating the transaction under. Request it from the Settings page in your dashboard. Without approval, requests with direct: true return 422 NO_PROVIDER_AVAILABLE.
A LIVE API key (test keys can be used for integration but no real funds move).

Telegram Stars
Accept payments in Telegram Stars (XTR) — Telegram's native digital currency — directly inside Telegram. Use this when your customers already live in Telegram (bots, mini-apps, channels) and you want them to pay without leaving the app.

This rail uses a separate endpoint from the main Payments API and returns a real t.me/$invoice/... link you can attach to a message, inline keyboard, or mini-app button. The customer never sees a Vexutopia checkout page.

When Telegram Stars makes sense
Good fit
Telegram bot or mini-app selling digital goods
You want zero card friction (no 3DS, no chargebacks)
Your customers already hold Stars or top up easily
Small-amount sales (subscriptions, tips, role unlocks)
Poor fit
Customer is on a website, not Telegram — use card or regional rails

Fees and settlement
Telegram takes its cut at withdrawal time, not per transaction — no per-payment PSP fee appears on your transaction.
Vexutopia's platform fee on Stars is volume-tiered: 5% under $100K lifetime, 4% at $100K – $200K, 3% at $200K – $500K, and 2% above $500K. The same tier ladder applies to the Crypto On-Ramp rail; see Providers → Volume tiers for the full table.
Stars proceeds settle to your Vexutopia balance and are withdrawn like any other coin or network — you choose the destination wallet (TON included, alongside every other supported network) when you request a withdrawal. No payout wallet needs to be configured up front. The USD-per-star conversion you see in the create response is locked at invoice creation for accounting purposes — the amount you receive in your chosen coin depends on its rate at withdrawal.
Maturity: T+21. Each completed Stars sale becomes withdrawable 21 days after the customer paid, matching Telegram's customer-refund window. Until then the sale sits in the "Maturing" sub-line of your Stars balance card. Your dashboard's "Available to withdraw" figure is genuinely what's safe to pay out — it does not include Stars still subject to a customer refund.
$40 fee per Telegram-issued refund. Customers can ask Telegram Support to refund a Stars payment. When that happens we record the refund on the transaction, decrement your pending Stars balance by the refunded amount plus the $30 equivalent, and surface it on the Refunds & Chargebacks page so you can keep track of it alongside disputes on other rails.

Payment Providers
Vexutopia offers four core payment rails. Your customers see only the options available for their region and amount at checkout — no extra configuration required on your side (except where a rail is API-only, noted below).

1. Crypto on-ramp — global and regional providers that convert the customer's card/bank payment into crypto delivered to your payout wallet.
2. Regional payment methods — local rails (Brazilian PIX, Argentine card-to-card, Russian SBP, EU Open Banking, Chinese Alipay & WeChat Pay) that settle to your Vexutopia merchant balance.
3. Card payments — direct Visa/Mastercard acquiring. In onboarding — available only after KYB approval and a dedicated compliance review.
4. Crypto (direct) — the customer pays in cryptocurrency through a hosted flow you start from the API with crypto: true. Not listed on the standard multi-method checkout page; see Payments API — Crypto (direct).
Telegram Stars is a separate optional flow — see the Telegram Stars section below or the Payments API for the POST /v1/payments/telegram-stars endpoint.

United States — card billing

US billing addresses are accepted on the standard inline card flow — the customer enters their card on the Vexutopia-hosted card page (URL stays on vexutopia.com), the same as every other supported country. US billing has three extra requirements: the customer must be connecting from a US IP address (fraud control — see below), a billing state is required (the US acquirer MID rejects sessions without it), and the per-charge floor is higher — $10 USD minimum for US billing (the EU/global terminal stays at $7). All other terms (12.5% MDR, $5,000 payout floor) are identical.

Standard hosted checkout (most merchants): No merchant action is required — send the customer to the checkout_url returned by POST /v1/payments and our card page handles US billing inline (collecting the billing state and applying the US-IP check). Nothing US-specific to wire up on your side.

Embedded / direct-API integrators: US billing now works on the standard inline card endpoint (POST /api/v1/checkout/{sessionId}/card) and in the embedded iframe, the same as other countries — subject to the US-IP gate below. The dedicated US hosted-session endpoint remains available as an optional alternative if you'd rather mint the session server-side:

POST /api/v1/checkout/{sessionId}/card-hosted
X-API-Key: <your-live-or-test-api-key>
Content-Type: application/json

{
  "email": "buyer@example.com",
  "firstName": "Jane",

The session is reusable for ~3.5 minutes — after that we mint a fresh upstream JWT on retry.

Below $10 USD the call returns 400 AMOUNT_BELOW_MINIMUM with the US-specific floor in the message.

A POST request is sent to your webhook_url with a JSON body:

Look up orders by id, payment_id, or order_id — all three are the same value.

When customers switch payment methods on the hosted checkout (e.g. abandon card → pay with WeChat), we create internal child transactions to track each rail. Verifying signatures
Every webhook we send is signed with HMAC-SHA256 so you can verify it came from Vexutopia and hasn't been tampered with. The signature is sent in the X-Vexutopia-Signature header.

Header format
The header value contains a timestamp and a hex-encoded signature, comma-separated:

text
Copy
X-Vexutopia-Signature: t=1715164620,v1=4a1b9c8d…
t — Unix timestamp (seconds) when the signature was generated.
v1 — Hex-encoded HMAC-SHA256 of `{timestamp}.{rawRequestBody}`.
Where the signing secret comes from
Register a webhook endpoint via POST /v1/webhooks. The response returns a secret once, at creation time — store it in your secret manager. We never expose it again. That secret is used to sign every delivery to that endpoint.

cURL
Copy
curl -X POST https://vexutopia.com/api/v1/webhooks \
  -H "X-API-Key: vex_live_your_api_key" \
  -H "Content-Type: application/json" \
  -d '{
    "url": "https://yoursite.com/webhooks/vexutopia",
    "events": ["payment.completed", "payment.failed"],
    "mode": "live"
  }'

# Response (only time you'll see "secret"):
# {
#   "id": "we_…",
#   "url": "https://yoursite.com/webhooks/vexutopia",
#   "secret": "whsec_…",
#   "events": ["payment.completed", "payment.failed"],
#   "mode": "live",
#   "is_enabled": true,
#   "created_at": "2026-05-08T11:00:00.000Z"
# }
Endpoint mode (test / live / both)
Endpoints carry a mode that controls which traffic they receive. Defaults to "live".

• "live" — receives only payments made with live API keys.
• "test" — receives only payments made with test API keys (e.g. vex_test_…).
• "both" — receives both. Use the payload's livemode field to distinguish them in your handler.
How to verify
Read the X-Vexutopia-Signature header and split on the comma to get t and v1.
Compute HMAC_SHA256(secret, `{t}.{rawRequestBody}`) and hex-encode it.
Compare against v1 using a constant-time equality check.
Reject the request if |now - t| > 5 minutes to mitigate replay attacks.
Use the raw request body
The signature is computed over the exact bytes we sent. If your framework parses the JSON and re-serializes it before you hash, you'll get a mismatch. In Express, mount express.raw({ type: 'application/json' }) on the webhook route.

Node.js / Express — verification middleware
Copy
import express from 'express';
import crypto from 'crypto';

const app = express();

// Stripe-style: capture the RAW body for signature verification.
app.post(
  '/webhooks/vexutopia',
  express.raw({ type: 'application/json' }),
  (req, res) => {
    const header = req.header('X-Vexutopia-Signature') || '';
    const parts = Object.fromEntries(
      header.split(',').map((kv) => kv.trim().split('='))
    );
    const t  = parts.t;
    const v1 = parts.v1;
    if (!t || !v1) return res.status(400).send('missing signature');

    // Replay window: reject anything older than 5 minutes.
    const ageSec = Math.abs(Math.floor(Date.now() / 1000) - Number(t));
    if (!Number.isFinite(ageSec) || ageSec > 300) {
      return res.status(400).send('stale signature');
    }

    // The raw body is a Buffer when express.raw() is used.
    const rawBody = req.body.toString('utf8');
    const expected = crypto
      .createHmac('sha256', process.env.VEX_WEBHOOK_SECRET)
      .update(`${t}.${rawBody}`)
      .digest('hex');

    const ok =
      expected.length === v1.length &&
      crypto.timingSafeEqual(Buffer.from(expected), Buffer.from(v1));
    if (!ok) return res.status(401).send('bad signature');

    // Verified — now it's safe to JSON.parse and process.
    const payload = JSON.parse(rawBody);
    res.status(200).send('OK');
    // ...handle payload.event...
  }
);

app.listen(3000);
Python — verification helper
Copy
import hmac, hashlib, time

def verify_vex_signature(secret: str, header: str, raw_body: bytes) -> bool:
    parts = dict(p.split("=", 1) for p in header.split(","))
    t, v1 = parts.get("t"), parts.get("v1")
    if not t or not v1:
        return False
    if abs(int(time.time()) - int(t)) > 300:
        return False
    signing_string = f"{t}.{raw_body.decode('utf-8')}".encode()
    expected = hmac.new(secret.encode(), signing_string, hashlib.sha256).hexdigest()
    return hmac.compare_digest(expected, v1)

Embedded Card Checkout
Embed Vexutopia's card checkout directly on your site. Customers complete payment without leaving your domain. Card data never touches your server — it's captured inside our iframe and processed under our PCI scope.

Prerequisites
Your site must be approved by us (KYC complete for that specific domain).
We'll add your domain to the allow-list — embedding will not work from any other origin.
United States billing

The embedded iframe accepts cards for every country, including the United States — the iframe collects the required US billing state and submits like any other country. US billing has one extra requirement: the customer must be connecting from a US IP address (fraud control). If they aren't, the iframe shows a refusal screen instead of the card form. No special handling is required on your side. If you'd rather not render the card form in your embed for US customers, two optional alternatives:

Easiest — redirect to checkout_url: If you detect the customer's billing country is US on your form, skip mounting the embed and instead window.location = checkout_url using the value POST /v1/payments already returned. Our card page renders the form for US billing inline (with the billing-state field and the US-IP check applied).
Programmatic — mint a hosted session yourself: POST /api/v1/checkout/{sessionId}/card-hosted with the customer's email and billing details; the response payment_link is a vexutopia.com/pay/card/processing/... URL — redirect the customer there. See Card payments — US hosted session.
The hosted card page iframes the upstream acquirer under the Vexutopia domain — your customer's URL bar never leaves vexutopia.com.

US-IP requirement: US-billed card payments only succeed when the customer is connecting from a US IP address. Both options above (redirect to checkout_url AND minting a hosted session via /card-hosted) enforce this gate. If the customer is on a non-US IP, they see a refusal screen with a “back to checkout” link instead of the card form, and the API call returns 403 US_IP_REQUIRED. Test-mode API keys (vex_test_*) bypass the gate so you can integration-test from any region. Full rationale at Payments → US IP requirement.

Events
All five events accept the same callback signature. The snippet above wires up the three you almost always need (onSuccess, onFail, onCancel); add the other two if you want UI affordances for them.

onFail — payment declined or processing error.
onCancel — customer abandoned the form.
onReady — iframe loaded and ready for input. Useful for hiding a loading spinner.
onExpired — session aged out before completion. The customer needs a fresh session to retry.

Handling Errors
Here's how to properly handle errors in your integration:

JavaScript
Copy
async function createPayment(amount, currency) {
  const response = await fetch('https://vexutopia.com/api/v1/payments', {
    method: 'POST',
    headers: {
      'X-API-Key': 'vex_test_your_api_key',
      'Content-Type': 'application/json'
    },
    body: JSON.stringify({ amount, currency })
  });

  const data = await response.json();

  if (!response.ok) {
    // Handle the error based on the code
    switch (data.code) {
      case 'INVALID_API_KEY':
      case 'MISSING_API_KEY':
      case 'REVOKED_API_KEY':
      case 'EXPIRED_API_KEY':
        throw new Error('Check your API key configuration');
      case 'VALIDATION_ERROR':
        throw new Error(`Invalid request: ${data.error}`);
      case 'NO_PROVIDER_AVAILABLE':
        throw new Error('No provider available for this currency/country/method');
      case 'RATE_LIMITED': {
        // Honour Retry-After (in seconds) and retry once
        const retryAfter = Number(response.headers.get('Retry-After') ?? '1');
        await new Promise(r => setTimeout(r, retryAfter * 1000));
        return createPayment(amount, currency);
      }
      case 'PROVIDER_UPSTREAM_UNAVAILABLE':
        // Safe to retry with backoff
        throw new Error('Provider temporarily unavailable, retry shortly');
      default:
        throw new Error(data.error);
    }
  }

  return data;
}
Rate Limiting
The API enforces rate limits to ensure fair usage. When you exceed the limit, you'll receive a 429 response.

Default Limits
• 300 requests per minute, per API key (sliding window).
• Applies identically to test-mode and live-mode keys.
Retry-After Header
On a 429 response, the API returns a Retry-After header containing the number of seconds to wait before retrying. Honour this value rather than retrying immediately.

Need Help?
If you encounter persistent errors or need assistance, contact our support team at support@vexutopia.com with the timestamp of the failing request and the returned code value.

How whitelabel works
As a whitelabel reseller you run your own merchants under your brand. You bring the merchants and set their pricing; we handle the payment processing, settlement and compliance behind the scenes.

The merchants you sign up are called sub-merchants. You decide what to charge each one. Each payment comes at an agreed cost to you; you set your own price on top and keep your fee on every payment they take. You never touch a customer's card or wait on a processor — that's our side.

The loop
1
Onboard a merchant

Add the merchant and set your fee — the markup you add on top of your cost. They start as pending.
2
We approve them

We review the merchant. Once approved they go active.
3
Connect their checkout

Add their website and generate an API key, then hand it to them (or their developer) to plug into their checkout.
4
They take payments — you earn

As they process payments, your balance fills up. You withdraw it, pay each merchant their share, and keep your margin.
Everything for a reseller lives in your Reseller dashboard (the “Reseller” item in your sidebar). The normal merchant pages — Customers, Transactions, Balances — stay empty, because you don't take payments directly. Your sub-merchants do.
Two numbers to know
Pricing comes down to two numbers, both on the pricing screen for every payment type:

Your cost — your all-in cost to run that payment, shown for you. It's a single bundled number: everything needed to take the payment is already included, so you never add anything to it. We agree it with you up front.
Your fee — the markup you add on top of your cost. This is your profit; your merchant pays your cost plus this.

Why approval?
Every merchant that processes through us has to be reviewed, the same way your own account was. It protects you and us: it keeps the payment networks happy and keeps bad actors off the platform. You're still the one who signs them up and sets their price — we just sign off before they go live.

Editing a merchant later
On each merchant's card you'll see Edit rates (change what you charge them) and Sites & keys (add their website and generate API keys). You can change rates any time.

2. Pricing & your margin
You set your fee per payment type. Your merchant pays your cost plus your fee.

The number you enter is your fee — the markup you add on top of your cost. Your merchant pays your cost plus your fee. So your profit is simply the fee you set.
What each box shows
When you onboard or edit a merchant, every payment type has a fee box. Around it we show two things so there's nothing to work out:

cost X% — your all-in cost for that payment type. This is one bundled number; you don't need to think about what makes it up.
merchant pays Z% — what your merchant is actually charged: your cost plus the fee you enter. Updates live as you type.
Example
Say your cost on cards is 12.5%, and you set your fee to 5%:

Your cost (shown for you)	12.5%
Your fee (what you enter)	5%
Merchant pays	17.5%
You enter 5 — just your fee, not the total. Your merchant is charged 17.5% (your 12.5% cost plus your 5% fee), and the 5% is yours. Set your fee to whatever you like; there's no cap.

Your cost is a single all-in number — everything needed to run that payment type is already included. You never have to add anything to it yourself; just choose your fee, and the “merchant pays” figure shows the merchant's real rate.
Different fees per merchant
You can charge each merchant a different fee, and a different fee per payment type (cards vs crypto, etc.). Change it any time from Edit rates on their card. You can only price the methods we've enabled for you — if one you want is missing, ask us to add it.

5
Hand it over

Send the key to the merchant or their developer to plug into their checkout. From their customers' point of view, the whole payment experience is yours — they never see us.
Treat the Live key like a password. Anyone with it can create real charges on that merchant's account. Share it over a secure channel, and generate a fresh one if it ever leaks.
What the developer does with it
The key plugs into our standard payments API — the same one any merchant uses. Your merchant's developer can follow the API documentation to create payments, set up webhooks and so on. Nothing about the integration is reseller-specific; the key simply belongs to that sub-merchant.

Each payment type still needs to be switched on for the merchant's site (for example, cards or crypto). We enable these during review, the same as for any merchant. If a method isn't showing, let us know and we'll turn it on.

4. Getting paid
We settle the full amount for all your merchants to you. You then pay each merchant their share and keep your markup.

Your balance
The Overview page in your reseller dashboard shows your balance across every sub-merchant, combined. It updates automatically as your merchants take payments. Funds become withdrawable once they clear the processing settlement window (this varies by payment type — cards take a few days, some methods are instant).

To actually withdraw, use the Balances page in your dashboard and request a payout there — it draws on the same combined balance.

Your statement
The Statement page breaks your balance down merchant by merchant. For each one it shows:

Owed to sub — what you owe that merchant.
Your markup — what you keep on their volume.
Settled to you — the two added together, which is what we paid you for that merchant.
You pay your merchants yourself, on your own schedule, however you normally would. We don't pay your sub-merchants directly — we pay you the whole amount, and the Statement tells you exactly what each one is owed.
If a payment is reversed
If one of your merchant's customers charges back or refunds a payment, the fee for that comes off your balance — since you're the one we settle with. Keep that in mind when you decide how much to pay each merchant.

FAQ
Quick answers to the questions resellers ask most.

When I set a rate, do I enter the total or just my markup?

Just your fee — the markup you add on top of your cost. So if your cost is 12.5% and you want to make 5%, enter 5. Your merchant is charged your cost plus your fee, which the screen shows live as “merchant pays”. See Pricing.
Why are my Customers, Transactions and Balances pages empty?

Because you don't take payments directly — your sub-merchants do. Everything for you lives in the Reseller section of your sidebar, not the normal merchant pages.
How long does merchant approval take?

It's a quick manual review on our side. You can add the merchant and create a test key right away; the live key unlocks once they're approved.
Can I charge different merchants different rates?

Yes — and a different rate per payment type. Set whatever fits each merchant, and change it any time from Edit rates on their card.
Do my merchants’ customers see Vexutopia?

No. The checkout shows your brand, not ours. Your customers never see who's processing behind the scenes.
How do I get paid, and how do my merchants get paid?

We settle the full amount to you. You withdraw from your Balances page, then pay each merchant their share yourself — the Statement shows exactly what each one is owed and what you keep.
I lost an API key — what now?

Generate a new one from the merchant's Sites & keys. Keys are shown once for security, so there's no way to see an old one again. You can keep more than one key per merchant.
Something isn’t working or a payment method is missing.

Send us the details and we'll sort it. A missing payment method usually just needs to be switched on for that merchant during review.
